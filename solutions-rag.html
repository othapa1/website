Knowledge Retrieval

Knowledge retrieval sits behind some of the highest-value workflow bottlenecks — support triage, document review, internal Q&A. Systems fail in production not because retrieval is hard, but because access control and quality measurement are treated as afterthoughts.

We design retrieval as a permission-aware system — not a vector search layer bolted onto a prompt.

Retrieval is not just about relevance. It must enforce access boundaries, measure quality, and remain observable under real usage.

The common failure mode: access control handled with post-retrieval filtering, retrieval quality guessed rather than measured, and no alerting when the system degrades. These gaps matter most in multi-tenant systems and regulated environments — exactly where retrieval systems are most valuable.

What we build

  • Ingestion pipelines with structured metadata and permission extraction at ingest time
  • Chunking strategies aligned to document structure and role-relevant boundaries
  • RBAC-aware retrieval integrated with identity systems — access enforced at query, not filtered after
  • Evaluation harnesses with recall/precision baselines and adversarial query regression

Production properties

  • Tenant and role-based access enforced at query time — zero cross-tenant document exposure
  • Retrieval audit trail aligned to compliance and data access requirements
  • Monitoring for recall drift and latency across tenant partitions
  • Runbooks and ownership documentation delivered at handoff
Representative outcome Zero permission violations in 6 months of production use across a regulated multi-tenant platform. Retrieval latency held under 380ms at p95 across 40,000+ indexed documents. Internal team assumed full ownership at handoff.

Without this architecture

  • Sensitive data exposed across tenants when access control relies on post-filtering
  • Retrieval quality degrades over time without recall baselines to detect it
  • No visibility into latency, drift, or failure modes at the retrieval layer
  • Compliance exposure when retrieval decisions can't be audited

If retrieval is already in use, the risk isn't retrieval — it's access control and correctness under real conditions.
